The Journey That Brought Me Here

Teaching, learning, and becoming.

I never thought I would become a teacher.

Growing up, I imagined myself doing many different things. At one point, I wanted to be a reporter. Then I thought about becoming a presenter. I even dreamed of becoming the Minister of Women's Empowerment and Child Protection. Looking back, those dreams may seem quite different from one another, but perhaps there was something connecting them: I have always been interested in people, communication, and making a difference.

I've always enjoyed public speaking, even though I'm naturally more of an introvert. I don't necessarily seek out crowds, but I enjoy sharing ideas, having meaningful conversations, and connecting with people. I also tend to adapt quite quickly when life takes me somewhere unexpected.

And life did take me somewhere unexpected.

I studied in Palembang and spent two years working there. After that, I decided to return to my hometown, Baturaja. At the time, I thought I would try applying for a teaching position.

My background wasn't originally from a teacher-training program, but I wanted to learn something new. And, perhaps in a very classic way, I also thought that becoming a teacher might help me learn how to be a mother someday. Yes, that was actually one of the reasons. :)

So I applied.

Around thirty people took the test, and only two of us passed. I was one of them.

Then came my first day in the classroom.

I honestly didn't know what to expect. But something surprised me. The students were excited to meet me, and they seemed genuinely happy to have me as their teacher. I found myself enjoying the classroom too.

What I initially thought might just be something I would try for a while slowly became something much bigger.

And somehow, I'm still here.

Five years into teaching, I realized that I didn't just want to teach based on what I had experienced. I wanted to understand education more deeply. So I decided to continue my education and pursue a Bachelor's degree in English Education.

Going back to being a student while already being a teacher gave me a different perspective. I could connect what I learned in class with what I experienced in my own classroom. Theory was no longer something that existed only in textbooks. I could see it in real students, real challenges, and real moments of learning.

Over time, I realized that what I love most about teaching isn't simply standing in front of a class and explaining a lesson.

I love being able to share things. I love listening. And I love being someone the students feel comfortable talking to.

When a student feels safe enough to tell me a story, ask a question, or simply share what is on their mind, I feel that something meaningful has happened.

Sometimes, the most important lesson isn't the one written on the board.

Teaching has also taught me patience.

Every child learns differently. They have different abilities, personalities, backgrounds, and ways of understanding things. Not every student will learn at the same pace, and that has taught me to be more patient, to listen more carefully, and to remember that education is never a one-size-fits-all journey.

Perhaps that's one of the reasons I continue to enjoy this work. Every day brings something I didn't expect. Every student teaches me something I didn't know I needed to learn.

And perhaps that's why I still want to keep learning.

Teaching has made me realize that there is always more to understand, more to explore, and more ways to contribute. I want to keep growing; not only as a teacher, but also as a person.

I don't know exactly where this journey will take me next. And maybe that's okay.

For now, I'm grateful for where the journey has brought me, and curious about where it will lead me next.

I just hope Allah guides every step of the way and is pleased with this journey.
